    or

    You can change the name and the event at any time from the bar at the top.

    Your music never leaves this computer. Files are read straight from your disk, and nothing is uploaded.

    • Choose the level at the top first. That sets the time you’re aiming for, and the big timer turns green when you are inside it.
    • The row of blocks is your song list. Click one to work on it. To change the order, drag its block left or right — or use Move earlier and Move later underneath, which is how to do it on a touchscreen, where dragging doesn’t work.
    • The bar underneath shows the real timing. Where blocks overlap, that’s a blend. The shaded stripe is the length you’re allowed.
    • Click anywhere on that bar to move the marker, then press Space to listen from there.

    Save project keeps your editing work, but not the songs themselves, so after loading a project you add the music files again. See Saving above for the details.

    • Cut where a line of the music finishes, not in the middle of one. If you can hear the join, it’s usually in the wrong place.
    • Set Blend into previous to 1 or 2 seconds to ease from one song into the next. Leave it at 0 for a sudden, dramatic change.
    • Line up this join shifts the cut by up to a couple of seconds to somewhere the music can take it, and tells you if that made the program longer or shorter. When both songs have a steady beat it puts them on it, which is what stops a blend sounding like a stumble.
    • Piano and a lot of orchestral music have no steady beat to find. There the button looks for the end of a phrase instead — the breath between one musical line and the next — and says that is what it did. Cutting there is what makes a join sound intended rather than accidental.
    • If it can’t find either, it says so and changes nothing, and you place the cut by ear with I and O. Ctrl+Z undoes it either way.
    • Even out the volume sets every song so they all sound about equally loud. Records are mastered at wildly different levels, and the rink’s sound desk is set once and left, so a quiet cut in the middle of your program simply disappears. Use it once your cuts are roughly right, and again if you re-trim, because it measures the part you kept.
    • The Volume slider on each song adjusts it by hand afterwards. Evening out is a starting point, not the last word — a gentle opening is often meant to be softer than the finish.
    • A small Fade in — about half a second — stops the music starting with a pop.
    • A Fade out of 2 or 3 seconds lets the ending finish gently instead of stopping dead.
    • Blending makes the program shorter, because the two songs overlap. Watch the timer as you move the slider.
    • Aim for the middle of the green range, not the very edge, so a few seconds either way won’t matter. Timing starts at the first sound and stops at the last, so quiet bits still count.
    • Listen on proper speakers before the competition. Laptop speakers hide problems the rink’s system will not.

    Allowed times differ by level and can change from year to year, so check yours with your coach.

    Save project writes a small file that records your editing — which songs you used, where each one starts and ends, and every fade and blend. It is only a few kilobytes because it does not contain the music itself.

    • Because the music isn’t inside it, a project file can’t be played on its own, and it can’t be handed in at a competition. Use Make music file for that.
    • When you load a project, you have to add the music files again. Use Add files, or drag them onto the page — anywhere will do. Dropping them on the row of blocks adds them to the program at the same time.
    • On some browsers the editor can remember the songs for you, and offers Open the music again — one click for the whole program. It still asks your permission, and the music still never leaves your computer. If that button isn’t there, your browser can’t do it and Add files is the way.
    • Songs are matched by name, so if you hand it a different song with the same name it will say so rather than quietly building your program around the wrong music.
    • Songs are matched by their file name. As long as the names haven’t changed, everything reconnects and all your cuts, fades and blends come back exactly as they were.
    • Until the music is added, those songs show up in red and the program won’t play or export.
    • If you rename a music file, the link breaks. Rename it back, or remove the song and add it again.

    Keep the project file in the same folder as your music, and the two stay together.

    New starts a fresh program and asks for a name and an event again. Your loaded songs stay in the list, and nothing is ever deleted from your computer — but the current program is replaced, so save it first if you want to come back to it.

    You don’t need to save after every change — this browser remembers your work between visits on its own. Save project is for keeping a copy, moving to another computer, or going back to an older version later.
